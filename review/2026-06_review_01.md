**Reviewer's verdict:** Reject, or require Major Redirection.

**Overall:** "Bluff coated in 'honesty' — a low-novelty repackaging."

The author acknowledged and corrected the previous fatal error (the false claim
of second-order *pointwise* convergence on general meshes) and presents this as
an honest stance. But the resulting Draft v2 has become "merely a restatement of
well-known, existing FEM properties in idiosyncratic, obscure terminology
(Skin-o-hedron)," of highly questionable academic value.

## Criticism 1 — the core has zero novelty (Remark 5.2)

In Section 5 the author defines a bespoke "Skin-o-hedron discrete Laplacian," but
in Remark 5.2 (and Remark 6.3) admits:
*"Because the reference Δ_h is the linear FEM Laplacian..."*

So the actual substance of the supposedly lofty model is nothing other than the
lowest-order cotangent Laplacian (linear FEM), completely proven by Dziuk and
others in the 1980s. A grandiose "trace-free dual tensor" is introduced, but if
the final output equals existing linear FEM, then that tensor structure is "mere
complicated decoration (redundant overhead) that changes no result." Attaching a
new name (Skin-o-hedron) to an existing method and dressing it up as a new
geometric substrate is academic deception.

## Criticism 2 — the super-convergence is icosahedron-symmetry magic

In Observation 6.1 the author boasts of `O(h^3.68)` eigenvalue super-convergence
on a quasi-uniform icosahedral subdivision, left as Conjecture 6.2.

Strong objection: icosahedron-based recursive sphere meshes have extremely high
regularity and symmetry (strong grid orientation). That special higher-order
error terms cancel on structured, clean meshes to give *apparent*
super-convergence is elementary common sense in numerical analysis. To claim this
as a benefit of one's own theory is naïve. To prove the super-convergence is not
due to mesh-orientation topology, the author must show near-fourth-order
convergence on a *symmetry-broken but shape-regular* family (e.g. fully random
Voronoi/Delaunay). The current data merely observes an "icosahedron bonus stage."

## Criticism 3 — admitting pointwise divergence yet claiming usability

The author invokes Wardetzky et al.'s "No Free Lunch" theorem, admits pointwise
accuracy diverges as `O(h^{-1.15})` on irregular meshes, and shrugs that energy
(spectral) convergence suffices (Proposition 6.2).

Do not underestimate real simulation. For the Navier–Stokes and semiconductor
heat-flow domains the author lists in Part II, local pointwise values diverging
means local physical quantities blow up to infinity. However correct the total
energy (eigenvalues) looks, an operator where one grid point diverges to infinity
cannot be embedded in a fluid solver — it will instantly emit NaN and explode.
Listing physically unusable things as "Applications" is false advertising.

## Criticism 4 — Part II (S-parameter) is an empty poem of equations

Part II is an "idea sketch (poem)" beneath the level of review. It merely
transcribes existing electromagnetics / scattering Green's-function
representations (Colton & Kress 2013) in clean LaTeX, with no organic coupling to
the Skin-o-hedron. In Observation 10.1 the author rejoices that singular values
hit 1 and unitarity holds to `10^{-15}` — but for a lossless toy model, hitting
floating-point precision is *obvious*. No essential breakthrough, no concrete
physical discovery is described.
